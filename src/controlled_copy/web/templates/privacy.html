What this demo processes, and why

  • Connection data. To deliver the pages, the server processes your IP address and each request. The app counts failed access-code attempts and new sessions per IP address in memory, for at most an hour, to stop abuse; it does not write IP addresses to disk. The web server keeps no access log; its error log can contain an IP address in rare failures and is rotated at a fixed size.
  • One session cookie (cc_session, signed, HttpOnly) links you to your anonymous workspace. It is strictly necessary for the service you ask for (§ 25 (2) no. 2 TDDDG), so there is no consent banner. There is no tracking, no analytics and no content from other sites.
  • What you add and ask. Your documents, pasted text, questions, situations, and the answers and Studio outputs made from them are stored on the server (uploaded files in its data folder, everything else in the app's database), tied only to your anonymous session. Please use public or synthetic documents only, and do not upload personal or confidential data.
  • Logs hold event names, a shortened hash of the session ID, route templates, sizes, durations and token counts, never document text, questions or answers.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR. The legitimate interest is to let reviewers and visitors try this portfolio project, and to keep it secure and its costs bounded.

Who receives data

  • Hetzner Online GmbH, Germany: hosting of the server and its database.
  • OpenRouter, Inc., USA, routes the model requests. When you upload, the text of your source goes there to create its embeddings (in batches; a batch is sent again if the service was busy); for each answer, suggestion, Studio output or card, your request goes there with the most relevant passages (for a follow-up question, also the last two turns of the chat). OpenRouter forwards each request to a provider that hosts the model, for example OpenAI, Google or Anthropic, or an inference host such as Parasail or DeepInfra for the embedding model. Every request asks for zero-data-retention endpoints and refuses data collection for training. This is a transfer to a third country (USA); OpenRouter's privacy policy names the EU standard contractual clauses (Art. 46 GDPR) and adequacy decisions (Art. 45 GDPR) as safeguards.

No one else receives your data, and none of it is sold or used for advertising.

How long it is kept

Log out deletes your workspace and everything in it at once. Otherwise it is deleted {{ retention_days }} days after your last visit. Deleted data leaves the database file at its next checkpoint, and there are no backups. Rate-limit counters stay in memory for at most an hour.

Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and to object (Art. 21). The quickest way to erase your data is Log out. Because the demo is anonymous, please describe your session (for example a notebook title) when you write. You can also complain to a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), in particular in the EU member state where you live or work.

Answers are generated by AI and cite their sources; no decision about you is made by automated means.
